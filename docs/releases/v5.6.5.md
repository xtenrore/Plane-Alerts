# Plane Alerts v5.6.5 — Persistent-Volume Headroom Recovery

Plane Alerts v5.6.5 is an emergency Railway reliability patch for the Plane Alerts operational persistent volume.

## Production problem

The `/data/prediction_lab` volume reached its capacity even though MongoDB operational telemetry had already been moved off Atlas. Prediction Lab evidence that had already been validated, committed and pushed to the evidence branch was being retained on the volume with a `.synced` suffix. Those acknowledged copies accumulated until file-backed Prediction Lab writes began failing with `ENOSPC`.

## What changed

- A dedicated persistent-volume headroom guard now targets 128 MiB of free space.
- The guard may delete only `*.synced` files under the Prediction Lab raw spool and verified historical import archive.
- `*.synced` is the repository-acknowledgement boundary: the sync workflow creates that suffix only after the corresponding evidence has been validated and committed/pushed.
- Unsynced evidence is never deleted by the guard.
- Route-history SQLite, notification-history SQLite, state files and durable application/user data are never cleanup candidates.
- Recovery runs immediately when the sentinel network starts, before its initial delay, so a nearly-full volume can recover as soon as the fixed build starts.
- Headroom is rechecked every five minutes to prevent the volume from silently filling again.
- Cleanup is bounded and runs off the critical five-second aircraft-monitor path.

## Storage boundary

MongoDB remains authoritative for durable user/application state such as users, Telegram identity, locations, profiles, preferences/settings and configuration.

High-volume route history, notification lifecycle telemetry and Prediction Lab evidence remain on the Plane Alerts-owned persistent volume with bounded retention/headroom controls.

No retired external-agent resource or integration is restored.

## Prediction safety

This patch does not change trajectory, CPA, ETA, destination qualification, alert qualification, cancellation logic or alert timing.

## Production verification

After deployment verify that:

1. The persistent volume recovers substantial free space and remains below capacity.
2. `No space left on device` / `ENOSPC` Prediction Lab failures stop.
3. Sentinel file-backed evidence writes resume.
4. No Mongo space-quota errors return.
5. Unsynced evidence and both operational SQLite databases remain intact.
6. The five-second monitor cadence remains healthy.
